## The key model

`project.key = { root, scale }`: `root` is a pitch class 0..11 (C = 0),
`scale` one of

| id | name | intervals | harmony (chords) |
|---|---|---|---|
| `major` | Major | 0 2 4 5 7 9 11 | itself |
| `minor` | Minor (natural) | 0 2 3 5 7 8 10 | itself |
| `dorian` | Dorian | 0 2 3 5 7 9 10 | itself |
| `mixolydian` | Mixolydian | 0 2 4 5 7 9 10 | itself |
| `harmonicMinor` | Harmonic minor | 0 2 3 5 7 8 11 | itself |
| `minorPentatonic` | Minor pentatonic | 0 3 5 7 10 | minor |
| `majorPentatonic` | Major pentatonic | 0 2 4 7 9 | major |
| `chromatic` | Chromatic | all 12 | major |

The order of that table is the **wire order** (`SCALE_IDS`): the engine
gets a scale as its index (Task 3's `SET_KEY`). Append only.

- New projects, New, and every file older than schema 6 are in **A minor**
  (the house default; migrate v5 → v6).
- Changing the key is **one undo entry** ("Key") and marks the project
  edited. `Project.key` is replaced whole (`applyKey`), never mutated.
- Undo/redo restore it through `history/diffApply.ts` → `applyKey`.

### The engine hook

`state/key-actions.ts` `keyCommands(key)` is the only builder of the
engine's key command. It is sent by `applyKey` (so `setKey` and undo) and
appended by `project/commands.ts` `projectCommands` after the transport
commands (so load, audio restart and export). It returns
`[SET_KEY, root, SCALE_IDS index]` (plan 26 Task 3; op 60). The engine keeps
the key per rack (`Synths::set_key` → `Rack::set_key`) for the Chord
module's In key; `RESET_ALL` puts it back to A minor. See
[modular.md](modular.md#note-modules-chord-20-arp-21).

## scales.ts

Pure, no Svelte. Its header lists the API; the rules worth knowing:

- `inKey(pitch, key)`, `isKeyRoot`, `scaleDegree` (0-based, or null).
- `nearestInKey(pitch, key, dir?)`: inside 0..127. Without `dir`: the
  nearest in-key pitch, **a tie goes up** (C#4 in A minor → D4). With
  `'up'` / `'down'`: the first in-key pitch that way, falling back to the
  other way at the 0..127 edge.
- **Two degree spaces.** `scaleDegree(pitch, key)` is the degree in the
  key's own scale (0..4 for a pentatonic, 0..11 for chromatic);
  `harmonyDegree(pitch, key)` is the degree in its *harmony* scale (always
  0..6). Chord maths (`diatonicChord`, `degreePitchClass`) only takes
  harmony degrees: in A minor pentatonic, E is scale degree 3 but harmony
  degree 4. Never pass one where the other is expected.
- `diatonicChord(key, degree, seventh)`: thirds stacked on harmony degree
  `degree`, as semitones above the degree's note (A minor degree 0 →
  `[0, 3, 7, 10]`, Am7). Pentatonics use their parent scale, so chords
  always stack real thirds; chromatic uses major (*my call*: there is no
  diatonic harmony to follow). Harmonic minor gives the textbook chords,
  i = Am(maj7) and III = Cmaj7♯5 included: correct, if harsh for house;
  pick a fixed type (m7) when they are not wanted.
- `CHORD_TYPES` (wire order, the engine Chord module's type index): maj,
  min, 7, maj7, m7, m9, sus2, sus4, dim, In key. `voiceChord(rootPitch,
  type, inversion, key?)` gives MIDI pitches, ascending, dropping any
  outside 0..127. In key snaps the root to the **key's own scale** (ties
  up), then plays the diatonic **seventh** on that note's harmony degree
  (plan 26: house chords are 7ths and 9ths). So a pentatonic key only builds
  chords on its five notes, with the parent scale's quality (A minor
  pentatonic: B → C, Cmaj7, never Bm7♭5); chromatic, where every note is
  "in", snaps to its harmony (major) scale instead. The fixed types play on
  the root as given. Inversion 0..3 moves the lowest
  min(inversion, n − 1) notes up an octave.

**Parity with the engine.** The Rust mirror (Task 3) cannot run TS, so
`scales.fixture.test.ts` writes `engine/tests/fixtures/scales.json` from
scales.ts (the scale and chord tables with their wire indexes,
`nearestInKey` in all three modes for every scale × root over the 0..127
edges and a middle octave, every diatonic triad/seventh, and ~1900
`voiceChord` cases) and fails when the committed file is stale.
Regenerate after changing the maths:
`UPDATE_FIXTURES=1 npx vitest run app/src/music/scales.fixture.test.ts`.
The Rust side is `engine/src/rack/modules/theory.rs` (scales, harmony,
chord tables, `nearest_in_key` without a direction, the diatonic seventh,
`voice_chord`); its tests read the fixture (`include_bytes!`, so a
regenerated file is picked up on the next `cargo test`) and check the tables
and every `nearest`, `diatonic` and `voicings` row. In key snaps the root to
the key's own scale (a chromatic key: to major), then stacks the seventh on
that root's harmony-scale degree, as `voiceChord` does.

## Piano roll

**Key: A min ▾** (toolbar, `pr-key`) opens `KeyCard`, which holds every
key tool (Task 1 review #2/#3: out of the toolbar, so Quantize and Clear
keep their labels at 1280 px): a root select (named in the Note names
style, so solfège shows "La min") and a scale select, Snap to key,
Transpose… and Fit to key. Each key pick is `setKey` at once; the card
stays open after every action until Esc or a press outside (after a Fit
that leaves nothing out of key, the focus moves to the card, so Esc still
closes it). While Snap to key is on, the Key button shows a small magnet
(`pr-key-snap-badge`).

**Highlight.** With a key other than chromatic, the note grid's row shading
follows the key instead of the black/white keys: **out-of-key rows** are
dimmed (`--well` at 60 %, deeper than a black key's 45 %), **root rows**
are tinted in the track colour (16 %), in-key rows are plain. A chromatic
key keeps the classic black-key shading and tints nothing
(`prMath.keyRow` → null).

**Snap to key** (`pr-snap-key` in the Key card, a toggle; a saved UI pref,
off by default, not in the project): pitches follow `prMath.movedPitch`:

- a **drawn** note lands on `nearestInKey` of the clicked row (ties up);
  the drag that follows stretches it and never changes its pitch ("Draw
  and stretch" below). When the snapped pitch already has a note at that
  start (or the click is past the end of a note shorter than the cell),
  that note is selected and dragged (moved, counting from the clicked row)
  instead: a draw never stacks a second note on one (review #1; `addNote`
  refuses an exact duplicate, same pitch and start, anyway);
- a **dragged** or **nudged** (↑ / ↓, Shift = octave) note goes to the
  first in-key pitch *in the direction of the move* (`'up'` / `'down'`), so
  a one-row move never snaps back to where it started; a time-only drag
  **or ← / → nudge** (review #8: one rule) puts an out-of-key note on its
  nearest in-key pitch;
- Alt still only turns off the time snap. There is no note paste in the
  roll yet; a future paste should go through `movedPitch` too.

**Transpose…** (`pr-transpose` in the Key card expands `TransposeCard`,
`transpose-pop`): a −12..+12 semitone slider and Apply. It moves the
selection, or every note when nothing is selected (as Quantize), as a
plain semitone shift (Snap to key does not apply). The group stops at 0 /
127 keeping its shape, and the readout says so ("Moved +7 (top of range)";
"Already at the top of the range: nothing moved"; review #6). One
"Transpose" undo entry; 0 is a no-op with no entry.

**Fit to key** (`pr-fit-key` in the Key card): the selection, or every note
of the pattern (past-the-end ones too) when nothing is selected (review #7,
like the other tools), to `nearestInKey` (ties up). One "Fit to key" undo
entry; disabled (and a no-op with no entry) when nothing it acts on is out
of key, e.g. any chromatic key. Its label names its target the way
Quantize's does ("Fit 1 sel." / "Fit all"): a note just drawn stays
selected, so it is easy to fit only that one. The readout says how many
notes moved.

**Stacked notes merge** (review #1). Transpose and Fit to key can move a
note onto another one at the same pitch and start (G# + A at one start →
A + A). Inside the same undo entry such a stack becomes one note
(`pattern-actions` `mergeStacked`): the longest is kept (the first on a
tie) with the stack's highest velocity, and the readout adds "merged N".
A move with **Snap to key** on can stack selected notes too (G# + A
nudged or dragged: both land on A): the roll runs the same merge
(`mergeStackedNotes`) at the end of the drag (inside its gesture) or right
after the nudge (in the same key task), so it is part of the move's own
"Move notes" undo entry. Without Snap a move never merges: putting a note
on another is then what the pointer asked for.

**Chord stamp** (plan 26 Task 2). The toolbar's **Chord** toggle
(`pr-chord`, aria-pressed) turns chord mode on; its **▾** (`pr-chord-opts`)
opens `ChordCard`: the chord type (`pr-chord-type`, `CHORD_TYPES`, In key
by default) and the inversion (`pr-chord-inv`, root position ..
3rd). Picking either also turns chord mode on (*my call*: you pick a chord
to place it) and hands the focus to the roll's grid (`releaseSelect`),
which closes the card: the next click places the chord and Esc leaves
chord mode at once. The type and inversion last the session (like the
grid size); chord mode itself starts off whenever the roll opens.

- **Ghost.** In chord mode, hovering an empty cell of the grid shows the
  chord there: one faint, dashed note per pitch, one grid cell long from
  the cell's start (Alt: unsnapped). The readout names it ("In key · A3 C4
  E4 G4 · click to place"); the canvas carries the pitches as `data-ghost`
  (e2e). No ghost over an existing note (a press there still moves or
  resizes it), past the pattern end, or with Shift held (a click
  box-selects). The roll keeps the pointer's point, not its cell, so the
  ghost follows a wheel scroll or zoom, and Alt / Shift pressed or
  released, without a pointer move. When every note of the chord would be
  past 127 the readout says "no room on the keyboard here".
- **Press** places every note of the ghost, velocity 100, as ONE "Add
  chord" undo entry (`addNotes`), and selects the whole chord (notes that
  were already there included). A chord note that would sit exactly on an
  existing note (same pitch and start) is skipped, not stacked; when
  that leaves nothing to add the readout says "That chord is already
  there" (no entry, no drag), and when every note is past 127, "No room on
  the keyboard for this chord here". Dragging on stretches every note of
  the chord together ("Draw and stretch" below); it never moves them, so
  the voicing never bends.
- **Pitches:** `prMath.chordStampPitches(row, type, inversion, key,
  snapKey)` = `voiceChord` in the project key. With **Snap to key** on, a
  fixed type's root first snaps to the key (`nearestInKey`, ties up: C#4 →
  D4, then D major as is, even if that has out-of-key notes); In key
  already builds on the key, so the toggle changes nothing for it.
- **The cap.** `addNotes` is all or nothing: when the pattern has fewer
  free slots than the chord has notes (`MAX_NOTES`, 1024), nothing is
  placed and the readout says "Pattern is full: no room for this 4-note
  chord (1024 notes max)".
- **Escape** cancels a drag first, else leaves chord mode, else clears the
  selection. Shift (box select, toggle) and right-click (delete) work as
  in draw mode.

**Draw and stretch** (owner request, branch `pr-drag`: "clicking and
dragging on a piano roll should stretch a note while it's dragged (all
modes, including chord)"). Every way the roll creates notes works the
same: a plain draw, a draw with Snap to key, and the chord stamp (the
roll has no paint / brush tool; the Acid tab's step grid writes notes
through its own path, `acid-actions.ts`, and is not affected).

- The **press** on empty grid creates the note(s) at once, one grid cell
  long: pitch and start from the cell (Snap to key applied, Alt: unsnapped
  start), as before.
- **Dragging** on (past the 3 px slop) stretches them: the end follows the
  pointer, on the grid line nearest it (Alt: to the tick), never shorter
  than one grid step, at most `MAX_NOTE_LEN`
  (`prMath.stretchLen`). The start never moves: a drag left of it keeps
  the one-step minimum. The pitch never changes: a vertical drag does
  nothing. (Before this, the drag after a draw *moved* the new note; to
  move it now, release and drag its body.) A chord's notes that were
  already there (skipped by `addNotes`) are stretched with the new ones.
  The length goes out through `updateNotes` once per animation frame like
  every roll drag; it may run past the pattern end, as a resize may.
- The **readout** shows the notes and their length while the press is
  held, in the selected-note format (`prMath.stretchReadout`: "C3 ·
  1.1.1 · 3×1/16"; a chord: "In key · A3 C4 E4 G4 · 1.1.1 · 1/4"). On the
  release the hover is refreshed at the pointer, so the chord ghost and
  the cursor are right without a pointer move.
- **Undo.** The whole press (create + stretch) is one entry, "Add note" /
  "Add chord": the gesture (`history/gestures.ts`) brackets it, and the
  first edit names it. No combined action was needed.
- **Escape** mid-drag removes the notes the press created (a chord's
  pre-existing notes get their length back); so does **Cmd/Ctrl+Z**
  mid-drag (`registerDragCancel`, the same cancel). Both run inside the
  still-open gesture, so it ends where it began: no entry, nothing older
  undone. Escape then stays in chord mode (a second Escape leaves it).
- A **click** with no drag keeps the default length (one grid cell). A
  press on an existing note still selects, moves or resizes it.

**Toolbar width.** The roll's toolbar stays one row at 1280 px
(`theory.spec` "layout at 1280", `chord-stamp.spec` at 1280 / 1440 /
1680): the key tools are in the Key card; Humanize… and the Chord toggle
are icon-only below 1440 px (each keeps its name as `aria-label`, and a
tooltip); Quantize (with its "N sel. / all" count) and Clear always show
their labels. The readout takes what is
left (it wraps only under 120 px) and ellipsizes a long text; its tooltip
has the whole of it. Its default help is short enough to fit at 1280
("4 notes · click to draw"); the tooltip adds the rest ("Shift-drag to
select, right-click to delete").
